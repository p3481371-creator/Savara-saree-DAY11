I

The thread
of gold

Zari is fine silver wire twisted around silk and dipped in gold. Pure zari stays soft for decades and never turns black.

II

Passed through
by hand

The shuttle crosses the warp thousands of times. Two weavers work in rhythm, one on the body, one on the border.

III

A border
is born

Pass by pass, the motif rises out of the red silk. A bridal border can take a full week of the loom's time on its own.

Bridal Trousseau

Three sarees,
three ceremonies

A South Indian wedding unfolds over days. We weave the trousseau as one set, so the colours speak to each other from the engagement to the reception, and every border is drawn for the bride who wears it.

1
The evening before

Nichayathartham

The engagement. Rani pink silk with a slim gold border, light enough for an evening of greeting family.

2
The auspicious hour

Muhurtham

The ceremony. Arakku red and gold, a temple-spire border and a fully woven pallu. The saree she ties the thaali in.

3
That night

Reception

The celebration. Emerald tissue silk that glows under lights, with zari running through the whole drape.
