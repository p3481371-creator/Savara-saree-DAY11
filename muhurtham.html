Bridal Trousseau

Three sarees,
three ceremonies

A South Indian wedding unfolds over days. We weave the trousseau as one set, so the colours speak to each other from the engagement to the reception, and every border is drawn for the bride who wears it.

1
The evening before

Nichayathartham

The engagement. Rani pink silk with a slim gold border, light enough for an evening of greeting family.

2
The auspicious hour

Muhurtham

The ceremony. Arakku red and gold, a temple-spire border and a fully woven pallu. The saree she ties the thaali in.

3
That night

Reception

The celebration. Emerald tissue silk that glows under lights, with zari running through the whole drape.

The wedding day

From dawn to the thaali

A traditional Tamil wedding in the order it happens, and which saree the bride wears for each part. Customs vary between families; we weave for all of them.

  1. The evening before

    Nichayathartham

    The formal engagement. The families exchange trays of fruit, flowers and the bride's new saree, which she changes into during the ceremony.

    Rani pink silkEngagement saree
  2. Early morning

    Vratham and Kashi Yatra

    Prayers for the couple, then the groom's playful pretend journey to Kashi until the bride's father persuades him to marry instead.

    Madisar or six-yardMuhurtham saree
  3. Mid morning

    Oonjal

    The couple sit together on a swing while relatives sing and circle them with coloured rice balls to ward off the evil eye.

    Arakku redMuhurtham saree
  4. Before the hour

    Kanyadaanam

    The bride sits on her father's lap as he gives her hand to the groom. She is then given the koorai saree to change into.

    KooraiGift from the groom's family
  5. The muhurtham

    Mangalya Dharanam

    At the auspicious hour the groom ties the thaali around the bride's neck as the nadaswaram plays and guests shower the couple with rice.

    Koorai or arakkuThe most photographed moment
  6. Right after

    Saptapadi

    Seven steps around the sacred fire, one for each vow. The pleats need to fall cleanly so the bride can walk freely.

    Same sareePleated by our draper
  7. That night

    Reception

    Lights, music and every relative at once. A lighter tissue silk keeps the glow without the weight.

    Emerald tissueReception saree
Bridal services

Everything around the saree

Border drawn for you

Sit with our designer to draw your border and pallu motifs. Your initials or the couple's names can be woven into the pallu.

Nine or six yards

We set the loom for a madisar or a six-yard drape from the start, with the pallu placed correctly for each.

Blouse tailoring

Cut from the saree's own blouse piece, with two fittings and zari piping that matches the border.

Draper on the day

A trained draper comes to your venue for the muhurtham and stays to repleat for the reception.

Trousseau box

Every bridal saree is packed in muslin inside a carved rosewood box, with a card naming its weavers.

Family sarees

Matching sarees for the bride's mother and sisters, dyed in the same batch so the colours sit together in photographs.
